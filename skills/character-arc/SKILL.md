# Character Arc

Use this skill when character behavior, development, motivation, or relationships need deliberate design.

## Workflow

1. Establish the character's current situation, external goal, internal need, fears, values, attachments, and constraints.
2. Separate characterization from deeper character logic: habits and mannerisms are surface; costly choices reveal the underlying person.
3. Identify meaningful contradictions that can coexist plausibly.
4. Define what pressure exposes, challenges, reinforces, or changes the character.
5. Map the arc through decisions and consequences rather than labels such as "becomes brave" or "turns evil".
6. Track key relationships as independent forces with their own goals and leverage.
7. Check that major changes have sufficient setup, resistance, and intermediate states.
8. Verify that dialogue, action, priorities, and emotional responses remain consistent with the character's current stage of development.
9. For flat arcs, verify that the character's stability meaningfully changes other people or the surrounding world.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce characters to trait lists or archetype labels.
- Do not make trauma automatically explain every behavior.
- Do not make character growth linear or frictionless.
- Do not overwrite established motivations without identifying what changed them.
- Avoid giving every major character the same voice, worldview, or emotional rhythm.
