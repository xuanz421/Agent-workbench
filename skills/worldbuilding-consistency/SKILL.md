# Worldbuilding Consistency

Use this skill to make setting elements behave like parts of one system rather than disconnected lore.

## Workflow

1. Identify the setting assumptions that materially affect the story: environment, resources, technology, supernatural rules, institutions, culture, conflict, and historical constraints.
2. Separate hard constraints from flexible conventions and unknown areas.
3. For each major rule, trace first-order consequences for ordinary life, power, trade, warfare, law, belief, class, travel, communication, and risk where relevant.
4. Check whether institutions and cultures have incentives that make sense under those conditions.
5. Look for contradictions between stated rules and observed outcomes in the story.
6. Prefer a small number of consequential rules over large quantities of decorative lore.
7. When adding a new power, technology, species, institution, or resource, check what existing assumptions it invalidates or pressures.
8. Record unresolved world questions instead of inventing answers without need.

## Guardrails

- Do not worldbuild every domain unless it affects the story.
- Do not assume real-world institutions would remain unchanged under radically different conditions.
- Do not add exceptions solely to rescue a contradiction without considering cost or precedent.
- Do not replace narrative mystery with encyclopedia exposition.
- Preserve established canon unless revision is explicitly requested.
