# Story Development

Use this skill to design or repair story structure before prose-level work.

## Workflow

1. Identify the story premise, central dramatic question, and intended emotional effect.
2. Separate confirmed story facts from optional ideas and unresolved decisions.
3. Define protagonist goal, opposing force, stakes, and the main source of escalation.
4. Map major turning points without forcing a specific beat-sheet framework unless it fits the work.
5. Check causal continuity: major events should follow through consequence, choice, revelation, or pressure rather than coincidence alone.
6. Verify escalation: later conflicts should become harder, costlier, or more revealing.
7. Check whether the climax resolves the central dramatic question and reflects the protagonist's accumulated choices.
8. Identify structural gaps, redundant sequences, dead subplots, or unsupported reversals.
9. When revising, prefer the smallest structural change that fixes the underlying problem.

## Guardrails

- Do not force every story into a fixed three-act or hero's-journey template.
- Do not confuse event quantity with narrative progression.
- Do not add twists that lack setup or consequence.
- Preserve established canon unless the user explicitly wants restructuring.
- Keep theme implicit in action, consequence, imagery, and character choice rather than turning it into exposition.
