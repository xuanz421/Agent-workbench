# Scene Writing

Use this skill for scene-level execution rather than whole-story architecture.

## Workflow

1. Establish viewpoint, location, time, immediate objective, and what has changed since the previous scene.
2. Give the viewpoint character a concrete short-term desire, question, fear, obligation, or decision.
3. Introduce resistance early enough that the scene cannot remain static.
4. Let information enter through action, observation, conflict, memory triggers, or dialogue pressure instead of undirected exposition.
5. Vary sentence rhythm, paragraph length, dialogue density, and sensory focus according to the scene's emotional speed.
6. Ensure at least one meaningful value changes by the end: knowledge, trust, danger, status, resolve, relationship, opportunity, or expectation.
7. End on consequence, decision, revelation, unresolved pressure, or earned stillness rather than an arbitrary cut.
8. During revision, remove repeated emotional explanation and redundant beats before adding more prose.

## Guardrails

- Do not start every scene with weather, waking up, or generic room description.
- Do not make all dialogue perfectly explicit; preserve implication and differing agendas where appropriate.
- Do not over-describe every object or bodily action.
- Avoid constant cinematic camera language unless the requested style calls for it.
- Maintain established POV and tense unless intentionally changed.
