# Continuity Tracking

Use this skill to protect long-form narrative consistency.

## Workflow

1. Build a compact continuity ledger from the material actually provided or available in the project.
2. Track at minimum: timeline, location, character state, relationships, knowledge, resources/items, injuries/conditions, promises, secrets, unresolved threads, and established world rules.
3. Mark each item as confirmed, inferred, unresolved, or contradicted.
4. Before drafting a continuation, identify which continuity facts constrain the next scene or chapter.
5. After drafting, compare new material against the ledger and flag contradictions before silently rewriting canon.
6. Distinguish intentional mystery or unreliable narration from accidental inconsistency.
7. When canon changes intentionally, record the new state and identify downstream scenes or summaries that may now be stale.
8. Keep unresolved hooks visible until they are resolved, abandoned deliberately, or reclassified.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent missing canon to make continuity appear complete.
- Do not treat inference as confirmed fact.
- Do not "fix" contradictions by silently changing earlier events.
- Do not force every planted detail to become foreshadowing; distinguish texture from promises.
- Prefer concise state tracking over exhaustive chapter summaries.
